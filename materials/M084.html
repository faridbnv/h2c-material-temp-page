PP-GF is glass-fibre-reinforced polypropylene, a semicrystalline polymer that carries light load well above its glass transition. Its HDT at 0.45 MPa is 144 °C. On the H2C a maker states that a hardened nozzle is necessary, and a maker recommends a brim to improve the adhesion of edges and corners.

Among the materials

Against PP-CF, PP-GF is 28 % less stiff, 27 % less tough in notched impact and 28 % stronger flat. Against PA12-CF, PP-GF is 60 % less tough in notched impact, 34 % stiffer and 19 % weaker flat. Among the materials, PP-GF has a high tensile modulus (XY) and a low elongation at break (XY) and density: each is in the top or bottom quarter of the materials that publish it. The rest of its key properties sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PP-GF typically reaches a tensile strength of 52.6 MPa at a modulus of 4.42 GPa, and breaks at 1.7 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 85 MPa at a flexural modulus of 3.71 GPa.

Thermal properties

Its products put HDT at 139⁠–⁠148 °C under 0.45 MPa and 117 °C under 1.8 MPa, against a glass transition of -5 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, Vicat softening is 138–164 °C, from one product and melting 158 °C, from one product.

Uses, limitations and chemicals

Finish and accuracy
  • PP can be postprocessed with sandpaper, razor blades, and a variety of other instruments. Wet sanding is recommended (due to airborne particles) but not necessary as the material doesn’t deform under the heat caused by friction. Prusa Research Prusament PP Glass Fiber by Prusa Polymers, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
