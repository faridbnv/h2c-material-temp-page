PVDF is polyvinylidene fluoride, a semicrystalline polymer that carries light load well above its glass transition. Makers sell it for electrical and electronic equipment and demanding applications, citing resistance to most chemicals and solvents, and a maker does not recommend food contact. On the H2C it prints at 250–265 °C, and a maker recommends a heated chamber but says no drying is needed.

Among the materials

Among the materials, PVDF has a high elongation at break (XY), HDT at 0.45 MPa, density and price: each is in the top quarter of the materials that publish it. Its tensile modulus (XY) and tensile strength (XY) sit in the middle half.

Mechanical properties

Printed flat (XY), PVDF typically reaches a tensile strength of 51 MPa at a modulus of 2.45 GPa, and breaks at 25 % elongation. In bending, its flexural strength is typically 50 MPa at a flexural modulus of 1.8 GPa.

Thermal properties

Its products put HDT at 158 °C under 0.45 MPa, against a glass transition of -34 °C. Semicrystalline, it carries light load well above its glass transition. As published, melting is 170 °C, from one product.

Uses, limitations and chemicals

What it is sold for

  • This material can be used for production of electrical and electronic equipment. Fillamentum Fluorodur, data sheet, p. 1
  • Rated for uses up to 130C – this filament can be used in demanding applications under the most extreme conditions. 3DXTECH FLUORX PVDF, maker's product page or guide, p. 1
  • PVDF is resistant to most chemicals and solvents, making it a great fit for applications which will be exposed to harsh environments. 3DXTECH FLUORX PVDF, maker's product page or guide, p. 1

What makers warn about

  • The usage for food contact application is not recommended. Fillamentum Fluorodur, data sheet, p. 1
  • The material should not be used for medical applications. Fillamentum Fluorodur, data sheet, p. 1
